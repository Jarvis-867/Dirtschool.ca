How to Estimate Earthwork for Small Contractors

April 2026  ·  11 min read  ·  Dirt School

Most small contractors lose money on earthwork jobs not because they're bad operators — but because they guessed on the estimate. Eyeballing a job and rounding up works until it doesn't. Then you're the guy who dug 400 yards and only charged for 250.

This is the actual process. Three parts: quantity takeoff, cost calculation, markup and profit.

Part 1: Quantity Takeoff

This is the math. How much dirt, how much material, how many truck loads. Get this right and everything downstream is solid. Get it wrong and you're already losing money before you've turned a key.

Calculating Cut Volume

For simple jobs, use the average depth method:

Volume (BCY) = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Average Depth (ft) ÷ 27

BCY = Bank Cubic Yards — material in the ground before it's disturbed. This is always your starting unit.

📐 Example: Residential Lot Cut

Lot: 60 ft × 120 ft, average cut depth 1.5 ft
Volume = 60 × 120 × 1.5 ÷ 27 = 400 BCY

Swell Factor — The One Everyone Forgets

When you dig material up, it expands. Common earth swells about 25%. That means 100 BCY in the ground becomes 125 loose cubic yards (LCY) in the truck. Forget this and you'll underestimate your truck count by 20–35%.

  • Sand and gravel: 10–15% swell
  • Common earth: 20–30% swell
  • Clay: 25–35% swell
  • Weathered rock: 30–40% swell
  • Blasted rock: 50–80% swell
LCY = BCY × (1 + Swell %)
📐 Example: Truck Loads

400 BCY of common earth, 25% swell
LCY = 400 × 1.25 = 500 LCY
Tandem truck payload = 12 LCY
Loads needed = 500 ÷ 12 = 42 loads

Shrinkage — When You're Placing Fill

When you compact fill, it shrinks. If you need 500 compacted cubic yards of fill in a berm, you need to haul in more than 500 LCY.

LCY to Import = Compacted Volume ÷ Compaction Factor (≈0.85 for common earth)

Part 2: Cost Calculation

Most operators only think about fuel. Your real cost includes ownership, maintenance, insurance, undercarriage wear, and fuel. Add those up and a mid-size excavator costs $80–$120/hour just to operate — before you pay yourself.

Equipment Hours

Hours = Quantity ÷ Production Rate

Starting production rates (these vary — track your own):

  • 20t excavator, general dig: 80–120 BCY/hr (hard ground = less)
  • D6 dozer pushing 50–100 ft: 200–350 BCY/hr
  • Motor grader, fine grade: 3,000–5,000 sy/hr
  • Tandem dump truck (1 mile haul): 3–5 loads/hr

Add 15–20% to your calculated hours for setup time, machine moves, and inefficiency. It's always there.

📐 Example: Excavation Cost

1,200 BCY excavation, 20t excavator at 100 BCY/hr
Hours = 1,200 ÷ 100 = 12 hrs + 20% buffer = 14.5 hrs
Equipment cost: 14.5 × $110/hr = $1,595
Operator wage: 14.5 × $45/hr = $653
Subtotal: $2,248

Don't Forget These

  • Mobilization — fuel and time to get there
  • Dump fees — if you're hauling material off site
  • Material supply — gravel, fill, road base at current supplier prices
  • Subcontractors — trucking, compaction testing, survey

Part 3: Markup and Profit

This is where most small contractors leave money on the table — or lose it entirely.

The most common mistake: Pricing for your costs, not for costs + overhead + profit. If you're just covering the job, you're slowly going broke. Overhead — insurance, accounting, loan payments, your time when not billing — has to come from somewhere.

Markup vs Margin — They're Not the Same

  • 20% markup: cost $1,000 → sell for $1,200 → profit = $200
  • 20% margin: cost $1,000 → sell for $1,250 → profit = $250

If you want 20% margin, you need to mark up by 25%. This trips people up constantly.

Quote Price = Total Cost ÷ (1 − Target Margin %)
📐 Example: Full Bid

Direct costs: $8,400 | Overhead: $3,600 | Total cost: $12,000
Target margin: 20%
Quote = $12,000 ÷ 0.80 = $15,000
Profit = $3,000 (25% markup on cost)

Typical Margin Ranges

  • Small residential (<$5K): 25–35% — mobilization is expensive relative to job size
  • Mid residential ($5K–$25K): 18–28%
  • Commercial site prep: 12–20% — more competition
  • Emergency/urgent work: 35–50% — charge for the availability
