How to Pass the Class 1 Road Test in Alberta

April 2026  ·  10 min read  ·  Dirt School

The Alberta Class 1 road test covers more ground than most people expect. It's not just driving on the highway — it's a pre-trip inspection, an air brake check, a backing maneuver, coupling and uncoupling a trailer, and a full road drive scored on observation, lane control, and traffic judgment.

Most fails come from one of three things: a rushed pre-trip that misses items, a backing maneuver gone wrong, or forgetting to do a G.O.A.L. (Get Out And Look) before reversing. All three are fixable with practice.

What the Test Actually Covers

  • Pre-trip inspection — full walk-around using the E-C-L-M-A-B sequence
  • Air brake inspection — static loss test, low pressure warning, pushrod stroke
  • Coupling and uncoupling — demonstrate safe fifth wheel procedure
  • Backing maneuvers — alley dock (90°), straight line, and offset
  • Road drive — city and highway, scored on observation, speed, lane use, intersections

The Pre-Trip: E-C-L-M-A-B

The pre-trip is where most people lose points — not on the road. Take 15–20 minutes. The examiner is watching whether you know what you're checking, not just whether you touch things.

E — Engine Compartment

Hood up. Check engine oil, coolant, power steering fluid, washer fluid, belts, hoses, battery, and look for leaks under the engine.

C — Cab Check

Mirrors, seat belt, horn, wipers, gauges, warning lights, fire extinguisher, emergency triangles, documents in cab.

L — Lights

Walk the full vehicle with lights on. Headlights (low and high), taillights, brake lights, turn signals both ends, hazards, clearance lights, reverse, plate light.

M — Mirrors

Both outside mirrors clean and properly adjusted. No cracks. Trailer mirrors cover the full length of the trailer.

A — Air Brakes

See below — this is its own sequence and takes 5–8 minutes done right.

B — Body Walk-Around

Tires, wheels, lug nuts, brake drums, fuel cap, exhaust, frame, suspension, fifth wheel, trailer connections.

Air Brake Inspection — The Numbers That Matter

The examiner will ask you to perform the full air brake check. Know these numbers cold:

  • Governor cut-out: 120–125 PSI
  • Governor cut-in: ~100 PSI
  • Low pressure warning: must activate at 60 PSI or higher
  • Static loss (engine off, brakes released): ≤3 PSI/minute for a combination vehicle
  • Applied loss (engine off, brakes applied): ≤4 PSI/minute for a combination vehicle

Automatic fail: Low pressure warning activates below 60 PSI, or an audible air leak is present at 1 metre distance. The examiner will know — don't ignore either.

Coupling — Don't Skip the Visual Check

After backing under the trailer and feeling the fifth wheel click, you must get out and visually confirm the jaws are locked around the kingpin. Every time. No exceptions.

  1. Back under slowly — stop when fifth wheel contacts the kingpin
  2. Connect glad hands (supply/blue first, then service/red) and electrical
  3. Raise landing gear — low gear first, then high
  4. Pull tractor forward gently against the jaws to test coupling
  5. Exit the cab and visually inspect the fifth wheel — jaws closed, no gap visible
  6. Release trailer parking brakes, test all trailer lights

Automatic fail: Driving away without getting out to visually confirm the fifth wheel is locked. Every examiner watches for this.

Backing — Alley Dock

The alley dock (90° back into a lane) is what trips people up most on the Class 1 test. You typically get two pull-ups before it counts as a fail on that maneuver.

  • G.O.A.L. first — get out, walk the area, check clearances. Examiners expect this.
  • Pull past the target opening so the trailer is already angled before you stop
  • Steer toward the side you want the trailer to go
  • Alternate mirrors constantly — both sides
  • If you're going wrong, take the pull-up early — don't wait until you're inches from a cone

Road Drive — What Gets You Failed

  • Running a red light or stop sign — automatic fail
  • Not checking mirrors every 5–8 seconds
  • No shoulder check before lane changes
  • Cutting right turns — trailer wheels must clear the curb
  • Not stopping fully at railway crossings (and don't shift in the crossing)
  • Riding the service brakes downhill — use engine braking
  • Following too close — 3–4 second minimum, more on highway

Test Day Checklist

  • Valid Class 5 Alberta driver's licence
  • Vehicle registration and insurance for the test vehicle
  • Current CVIP inspection sticker on the truck
  • Glasses or contacts if required on your licence
  • Payment for the test fee

Book through an Alberta registry agent. Bring your own vehicle — or arrange one. The truck must be a proper Class 1 combination (tractor + trailer) with air brakes.
