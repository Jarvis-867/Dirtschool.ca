How to Pass the Class 3 Road Test in Alberta

April 2026  ·  9 min read  ·  Dirt School

A Class 3 covers you to drive single unit trucks with a GVW over 11,794 kg — tandem dump trucks, concrete mixers, heavy straight trucks. If you're operating heavy equipment on highway construction and need to move your own materials, this is the licence you're after.

The Class 3 test is shorter than the Class 1 — no trailer coupling required, and backing is typically straight line or offset rather than a full alley dock. But the pre-trip and air brake inspection are the same standard, and the road drive scoring is identical.

Class 3 vs Class 1: If there's any chance you'll ever drive a semi or haul equipment on a float, get your Class 1 instead. The test is harder but the licence covers everything Class 3 does, plus more. Class 3 is the better choice if you only ever need a straight truck.

What the Class 3 Test Covers

  • Pre-trip inspection — full walk-around, E-C-L-M-A-B sequence
  • Air brake inspection — if the vehicle has air brakes (most do)
  • Backing — straight line back, possibly offset depending on examiner
  • Road drive — city and highway driving scored on observation, speed, lane use

No coupling/uncoupling. No alley dock. Those are Class 1 requirements.

Pre-Trip Inspection — E-C-L-M-A-B

Same sequence as Class 1. Don't rush it — examiners know when you're faking it.

E — Engine Compartment

Engine oil, coolant, power steering, washer fluid, belts, hoses, battery, leaks on the ground.

C — Cab Check

Mirrors, seat belt, horn, wipers, gauges, warning lights, fire extinguisher, emergency triangles, documents.

L — Lights

Headlights low and high, taillights, brake lights, turn signals front and rear, hazards, clearance lights, reverse, plate light.

M — Mirrors

Both outside mirrors — clean, properly adjusted, no cracks.

A — Air Brakes

Full inspection sequence — build pressure, test governor, test low pressure warning, static loss test, parking brake test, service brake test.

B — Body Walk-Around

Tires, wheels, lug nuts, brake drums, fuel cap, exhaust, frame, suspension, body condition.

What to Check on Tires

Tire defects are the most common thing examiners flag. Check every tire:

  • Steer axle tread: minimum 4/32" — use a quarter or tread gauge
  • Drive axle tread: minimum 2/32"
  • No sidewall damage, bulges, or exposed cord — automatic out-of-service
  • No dual tires touching — fire hazard, major defect
  • Valve stem caps present — missing cap is minor; damaged stem is major

Air Brake Inspection — Key Numbers

  • Governor cut-out: 120–125 PSI
  • Governor cut-in: ~100 PSI (compressor kicks back in)
  • Low pressure warning: must activate at 60 PSI or higher
  • Static loss (single vehicle, brakes released): ≤2 PSI/minute
  • Applied loss (single vehicle, brakes applied): ≤3 PSI/minute

Automatic fail: Missing the low pressure warning check, or an audible air leak at 1 metre. Both are major defects — the examiner knows the vehicle.

Backing — Class 3

For a straight truck, backing is fundamentally different from a combination vehicle — the truck goes where you steer it. The examiner will ask for a straight-line back and possibly an offset.

  • G.O.A.L. before every back — walk the area, check clearances. Do this every time without being asked.
  • Use both mirrors — don't just look over your shoulder
  • Slow and controlled — examiners mark off for excessive speed while backing
  • If you're going wrong, stop and straighten before you're committed to a mistake

Road Drive — Common Fails

  • Not scanning mirrors every 5–8 seconds — examiners watch your eyes and head movement
  • No shoulder check before lane changes — even if mirrors are clear
  • Cutting right turns — a tandem dump truck needs more room than a car
  • Railway crossings — full stop, don't shift in the crossing, look both ways
  • Following distance — 3–4 seconds minimum, more on highway and in wet conditions
  • Riding brakes downhill — engine brake, gear down, use service brakes to control, not sustain speed

What to Bring on Test Day

  • Valid Alberta Class 5 driver's licence
  • Vehicle registration and insurance for the test truck
  • Current CVIP (annual inspection) sticker on the vehicle
  • Payment for the test fee (book through an Alberta registry agent)
  • Glasses or contacts if required on your licence

Q Endorsement — Air Brakes

If your test vehicle has air brakes (most Class 3 vehicles do), you need the Q endorsement. This requires passing a separate written knowledge test at the registry before your road test. Study the air brake inspection sequence, the key pressure numbers, and brake system components.
