IUOE Aptitude Test — What to Expect and How to Pass It

April 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  Dirt School

The IUOE aptitude test stops more experienced operators than it should. Guys who've been running equipment for ten or fifteen years walk in cold and fail — not because they don't know the job, but because they haven't thought about fractions since Grade 10.

This is what the test actually covers, why people fail it, and how to fix that before you walk in the door.

Which Locals Use This Test

The aptitude test is used by IUOE locals across Canada and the US. In western Canada, the two main ones are:

  • Local 115 — British Columbia and Yukon
  • Local 955 — Alberta (Edmonton, Calgary, Fort McMurray, Edson)

The test format is similar across locals. The content differs slightly — Local 955 has more Alberta-specific context (H2S, oil sands, AIT), Local 115 focuses on BC regulations and highway/infrastructure work. The core four sections are the same everywhere.

The Four Sections

Section 1

Math

Fractions, percentages, ratios, area, volume, unit conversions, word problems. Grade 10 level — but written in test format, on paper, with a clock running.

Section 2

Mechanical Reasoning

Hydraulics, gears, levers, pulleys, weight distribution, basic engine systems. How machines work, not just how to operate them.

Section 3

Reading Comprehension

Short passages about safety regulations or procedures, followed by questions. Answer based on what the passage says — not what you know from experience.

Section 4

Spatial Reasoning

Shape rotation, pattern sequences, 3D views, net/unfolding diagrams. Visualizing objects in space — harder to self-study without diagrams.

Why Experienced Operators Fail

It's almost always the math section. Not because they can't do the math — they calculate cut depths, load weights, and fuel burn in their heads every day. The problem is test format.

Writing "3/8 + 5/12" on paper with answer choices in front of you is a different skill than doing the same calculation in your head while reading a grade stake. The brain operates differently under test conditions. If you haven't done written math in years, the first few questions can cause a freeze that eats your time and confidence.

The fix is simple: do the practice problems on paper. Not in your head, not mentally — on paper with a pencil. Ten days of that and the freeze doesn't happen.

The spatial reasoning section is the other common sticking point. People who are excellent at 3D thinking in the field — visualizing cut slopes, reading grade stakes, understanding how material moves — still struggle with diagram-based spatial questions because the format is unfamiliar. The solution is exposure: see enough of these questions and the format stops being confusing.

How Many Attempts Do You Get?

Most IUOE locals give you three attempts maximum. Fail three times and you're locked out for a period — sometimes years.

This is why preparation matters disproportionately here. You're not studying to improve a score by a few points — you're protecting your attempts. Walking in cold and failing is expensive in a way that can't be fixed by just trying again next week.

What a Realistic Prep Schedule Looks Like

  1. Days 1–3: Math refresher. Fractions, percentages, ratios, area/volume. Do every practice problem on paper.
  2. Days 4–5: Mechanical reasoning. Gears, hydraulics, levers, pulleys. If you understand how your machine works, this section should be manageable — but review it anyway.
  3. Days 6–7: Reading comprehension + spatial reasoning. Reading should be fast for most operators. Spatial needs diagram exposure.
  4. Days 8–10: Mock test. Full 60 questions under timed conditions. Check your score, identify weak spots, go back and review.
  5. Day 11–14: Targeted review of whatever you missed. Re-do the mock test if needed.

Two weeks is enough for most experienced operators. Some need less. The key is doing it actively — pencil in hand, not just reading.

The Day of the Test

A few things that help:

  • Read each question twice. Watch for "NOT," "EXCEPT," "always," "never" — these words flip the answer.
  • Do the easy ones first. If a question is taking too long, mark it and come back. Don't let one hard question eat five minutes of time.
  • For reading comprehension: answer based on what the passage says, not what you know from the field. They're testing whether you can follow written instructions — not your field experience.
  • For spatial: find one unique feature of each shape and track it. Don't try to visualize the whole object — anchor on one corner or edge.
  • Show your math work. If you make an arithmetic error you can catch it when you check. Answers in your head can't be checked.

After You Pass

You're on the dispatch list. Work comes to you through the union hall. You'll be placed with contractors, accumulating hours toward your endorsements. As an apprentice you're learning while earning union-scale wages.

In BC (Local 115): journeyman rates run $45–65/hr depending on classification and project type.

In Alberta (Local 955): industrial work pays $61–81/hr depending on equipment and tonnage. Non-industrial highway and municipal work runs $44–50/hr.

Either way — the card pays for itself fast. For an operator who's already got the seat time, the aptitude test is just paperwork. Treat it that way.

"The test gets you in the door. What you do with the seat time is on you."
