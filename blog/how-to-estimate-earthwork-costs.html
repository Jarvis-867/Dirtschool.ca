How to Estimate Earthwork Costs — A Practical Guide for Contractors

April 2026 · 8 min read · For contractors, foremen, and operators who want to understand where the money goes

Earthwork estimates are where most small contractors lose money. The math looks simple on the surface — dig it, haul it, fill it. But there are layers most guys miss. You end up winning the bid and losing your shirt on the job.

This is the process. Step by step. No software required.

1 Calculate Volume in Bank Cubic Yards

Start with the material in the ground. Bank cubic yards (BCY) means the volume before you touch it. Soil that hasn't been disturbed.

The formula: Length × Width × Depth ÷ 27

Divide by 27 to convert cubic feet to cubic yards. Example: a cut section 100 ft long × 40 ft wide × 6 ft deep = 24,000 cubic feet ÷ 27 = 889 BCY.

For irregular cuts or fills, break the area into sections and add them up. If you have cross-section drawings, use the average end area method. If you don't — get them.

2 Account for Swell

When you dig material out of the ground, it expands. Common soil swells roughly 25%. Rock can swell 30–40%. This matters because you're hauling loose cubic yards in the trucks, not bank cubic yards.

Formula: BCY × swell factor = Loose Cubic Yards (LCY)

889 BCY × 1.25 = 1,111 LCY going into the trucks.

If you're pricing the haul at bank volumes, you just underbid your trucking by 25%. That's a common mistake. Your trucks are moving loose material, not bank material.

3 Excavation Cost

This is machine time to break and load the material. Typical ranges:

Material TypeCost per BCY
Easy soil (loam, clay, sand)$6–8/cy
Mixed soil with cobbles$8–12/cy
Rippable rock (dozer rip + excavate)$15–25/cy
Hard rock (blast + excavate)$30–60/cy+

Access matters. Tight sites with limited swing room slow production and push costs up. Wide open cuts with good truck access drive costs down.

4 Hauling Cost

Hauling is where estimates fall apart the most. The cost per yard swings dramatically based on haul distance — because it's all about cycle time.

Cycle time = load time + haul out + dump + haul back + spot. A 25-tonne articulated truck making a 5-minute haul can do 10–12 cycles in a 10-hour shift. The same truck on a 30-minute haul does 3–4 cycles. Same machine, same operator — four times less production.

Haul DistanceTypical Cost
Short haul (under 1 km)$3–6/LCY
Medium haul (1–5 km)$6–12/LCY
Long haul (5–15 km)$12–20/LCY
Long haul with highway (15 km+)$18–30/LCY+

Remember: you're pricing haul in loose cubic yards. Don't forget to apply your swell factor before calculating trucking costs.

Also factor haul road maintenance. On a long project with heavy truck traffic, you'll spend real money grading and watering the haul road. It's not free. Add it.

5 Fill Placement and Compaction

Getting material to where it's going is only part of it. Placing it in lifts and compacting to spec takes machine time too.

  • Dozer spreading fill in lifts: included in most earthwork rates
  • Compaction with a pad foot or smooth drum: $3–6/cy depending on lift thickness and spec
  • Proof rolling: often a separate line item
  • Testing (density testing): budget $300–600/day for a testing lab depending on spec requirements

If the spec requires 98% proctor, budget more compaction passes and more testing. Highway and industrial specs are stricter than residential fills.

6 Add Contingency

Always add 15–20% contingency. No exceptions.

Unknown subsurface conditions. Unsuitable material that needs to be hauled to waste instead of used as fill. Weather delays. Change orders that expand the scope. A soft section that needs geotextile and crushed rock you didn't budget for.

If you don't need it, you come in under budget. If you do need it, you're covered. That's what contingency is for. Contractors who skip it are the ones who finish a job and wonder where the money went.

Common Mistakes That Kill Earthwork Margins

  • Not converting to loose yards for hauling. You're moving swell material in trucks. Price accordingly.
  • Ignoring haul road maintenance. Grading and watering a dirty haul road is a real cost on longer jobs.
  • Underestimating cycle time. Count your trucks and do the math on realistic cycles per shift before pricing.
  • Not accounting for unsuitable material. Some of what you dig isn't going into the fill — it's going to waste. That's extra haul you didn't price.
  • Estimating from the drawings without a site visit. Access, slopes, existing structures — they all affect production. Visit the site.

Typical All-In Cost Ranges

Job TypeRough All-In Range
Simple short-haul earthwork (good material, close dump)$15–25/cy
Medium complexity (mixed material, 3–5 km haul)$25–40/cy
Complex long-haul or rock (blast, long haul, tight access)$40–80/cy+

These are ballpark figures. Every job is different. Use them as a sanity check, not a quote.

If your number is significantly below these ranges, you missed something. Go back through the steps.
