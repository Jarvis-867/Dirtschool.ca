IUOE Local 115 vs Local 955 — What's the Difference?

April 2026  ·  5 min read  ·  Dirt School

Both are IUOE locals — same international union, different provinces, different work environments. If you're deciding which one to apply to, or wondering whether your BC card works in Alberta, here's the breakdown.

Local 115

British Columbia & Yukon

  • Based in Maple Ridge, BC
  • 14,000+ members
  • Highway construction, pipeline, mine sites, infrastructure
  • Seasonal work typical — May to October
  • Mountain terrain, camp-based jobs common
  • Non-industrial journeyman: $42–52/hr+
Local 955

Alberta

  • Based in Edmonton
  • One of the largest IUOE locals in North America
  • Oil sands, pipeline, highway, industrial construction
  • Year-round work available (especially oil sands)
  • Flat terrain, large-scale fleet operations
  • Industrial journeyman: $61–81/hr depending on classification

The Work Is Different

Local 115 (BC/Yukon): Heavily weighted toward highway construction through mountain terrain. You'll work narrow cuts on steep slopes, deal with variable ground conditions, rock work, drainage. The machines tend to be smaller relative to oil sands equipment. Camp jobs are common — especially in Yukon where remote projects run through summer. Graders, excavators, compactors, scrapers.

Local 955 (Alberta): Oil sands work is on a different scale entirely. Equipment you won't see anywhere else — massive mining shovels, 400-tonne haul trucks, large draglines. Pipeline work covers the province. Industrial construction (refineries, upgraders) is crane-heavy. The terrain is flat but ground conditions in muskeg and permafrost zones are their own challenge. Year-round work is more available than BC.

Wages — The Real Gap

This is where the two locals differ most significantly.

Alberta's industrial rates (oil sands, pipeline, plant construction) are substantially higher than BC non-industrial rates. A Group 2 operator (excavator, large loader, D9+ dozer) earns $64.24/hr on industrial work in Alberta vs. roughly $46–50/hr on highway work in BC.

However — BC highway operators often work extremely long hours during season. The annual income comparison narrows when you factor in 10–12 hour days, 7 days a week for 6 months.

Oil sands operators work rotating shifts year-round. Different lifestyle calculation entirely.

Can You Transfer Between Locals?

Yes — IUOE locals have reciprocal agreements. If you have your journeyman ticket with Local 115, you can work in Alberta under 955 jurisdiction. Your Red Seal (if you've written it) is interprovincial and recognized everywhere in Canada.

The practical reality: many operators work BC for the season, then pick up winter pipeline or industrial work in Alberta. The card travels.

Transferring formally from one local to another involves paperwork and dues — worth calling both locals directly if you're planning a move.

The Aptitude Test — Same Format, Different Content

Both locals use an aptitude test to screen applicants. The format is the same: math, mechanical reasoning, reading comprehension, spatial reasoning.

The content differs slightly. Local 955 prep should include Alberta-specific material — H2S awareness, AIT (Alberta Apprenticeship and Industry Training), oil sands context, cold weather operations. Local 115 prep focuses on BC regulations, WorkSafeBC, highway construction context.

The math and mechanical sections are functionally identical. If you've prepped for one, you're mostly prepared for the other.

"The card is the same union. The work is different. The wages are different. Choose based on where you want to live and work — not just the hourly rate."

Which One Should You Apply To?

If you're in BC or Yukon and want to stay — Local 115. If you want oil sands money and are willing to go to Alberta — Local 955. If you want maximum flexibility long-term — get your Red Seal and both locals become accessible.
