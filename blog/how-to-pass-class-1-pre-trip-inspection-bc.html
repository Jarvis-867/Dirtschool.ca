How to Pass the Class 1 Pre-Trip Inspection in BC and Yukon

April 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  Dirt School

Most people who fail their Class 1 road test don't fail on the highway. They fail in the first 10 minutes — during the pre-trip inspection.

The examiner gives you a vehicle and watches you walk around it. They're counting what you check, in what order, and whether you actually know what you're looking for. Miss enough items and you're done before you've touched the steering wheel.

This is the exact sequence. Memorize it.

In BC under MELT: You must pass two separate pre-trip assessments. Maximum 15 demerits per assessment. Each missed or incorrectly completed item = demerits. Don't walk in cold.

The 6-Step Sequence — E-C-L-M-A-B

Every pre-trip follows the same order. Use this acronym:

E — Engine Compartment

Under the hood first. Fluids, belts, hoses, battery, leaks.

C — Cab (Before + After Start)

In-cab checks before you start. Then start the engine and check gauges, lights, wipers, horn.

L — Lights

Circle check with lights on — headlights, signals, tail, brake, clearance.

M — Mechanical Circle Check

Full walk-around: front → driver's side → rear → passenger's side.

A — Air Brakes

Build pressure, test low-pressure warning, static loss test, pushrod stroke.

B — Brake Tests

Parking brake tug, trailer brake tug, foot brake response, breakaway switch, steering slack.

Engine Compartment — What to Check

  • Engine oil — pull dipstick, wipe, re-insert, check level and colour
  • Coolant — reservoir between MIN/MAX, no leaks on hoses or radiator
  • Power steering fluid — between MIN/MAX
  • Brake fluid — between MIN/MAX (below ¼ full = major defect, don't drive)
  • Washer fluid — sufficient level
  • Belts — proper tension, no cracks, no fraying
  • Hoses — secure connections, no kinks, no cracks, no leaks
  • Battery — no corrosion, secure mount
  • Under engine — no fuel, oil, or coolant puddles

In-Cab Checks

Before Starting

  • Seat adjusted, seatbelt functional
  • Mirrors adjusted and clean
  • Windshield clear — no cracks in driver's sweep area
  • Documents on hand: licence, registration, insurance, trip inspection report + schedule

After Starting

  • Oil pressure rises within seconds of start
  • Coolant temperature rises to normal operating range
  • Voltmeter shows charging (13.5–14.5V)
  • Air pressure builds to 100–130 PSI within 3 minutes at fast idle
  • Low pressure warning deactivates above 60 PSI
  • Wipers and washers work
  • Heater and defroster work
  • Horn, four-way flashers, interior lights all functional

Lights Circle Check

Turn on low beams, turn signals, clearance lights. Walk around and confirm:

  • Low beam headlights (both)
  • Left and right turn signals (front and rear)
  • Tail lights and clearance/marker lights
  • Licence plate light
  • Brake lights — press pedal, confirm all illuminate
  • High beams — confirm both work

Mechanical Circle Check

Move counterclockwise: front → driver's side → rear → passenger's side.

On Every Side

  • Mirrors — secure and clean
  • Exhaust — no leaks, securely mounted
  • Fuel cap — tight
  • Suspension — no broken springs, no loose U-bolts, air bags not cracked/deflated
  • Tires — proper inflation, no bulges, no exposed cord, adequate tread
  • Wheels — no cracks, no rust streaks from lugs, all nuts present
  • Duals not touching, nothing jammed between them
  • Mud flaps present, not rubbing tires

Fifth Wheel (If Pulling a Trailer)

  • No daylight between the fifth wheel plate and trailer apron — none at all
  • Locking jaws closed around the kingpin
  • Glad hands connected and locked (blue = service, red = emergency)
  • 7-pin electrical plugged and locked
  • Breakaway cable connected with enough slack for turns

Air Brake Inspection

  1. Build pressure to governor cut-out (~125 PSI)
  2. Fan brakes down — low pressure warning must activate at or before 60 PSI
  3. Emergency brakes apply automatically at ~20–45 PSI
  4. Rebuild pressure. Static loss test (brakes off): ≤ 2 PSI per minute
  5. Static loss test (brakes on): ≤ 4 PSI per minute
  6. Check pushrod stroke — must not exceed limit stamped on chamber

Major defects — do not drive: Air loss rate exceeded · Low pressure warning doesn't activate · Pushrod stroke over limit · Inoperative parking or service brakes

Brake Tests

  1. Parking brake tug: In low gear, parking brakes set — vehicle must not move
  2. Trailer brake tug: Apply trailer brakes only — trailer must resist
  3. Foot brake response: Move forward slowly, apply brakes — smooth stop, no pull
  4. Breakaway switch: Pull pin — trailer brakes must hold on tug test
  5. Steering wheel slack: ≤ 40mm free play before wheels respond

Common Instant-Fail Mistakes

  • Rushing through the engine compartment and missing items
  • Forgetting to check brake lights (requires a helper or wall reflection)
  • Skipping the fifth wheel inspection entirely
  • Not doing the air loss test — just checking pressure builds
  • Forgetting the breakaway switch test
  • Going through the sequence in the wrong order
  • Not naming components aloud (examiners want to see you know what you're checking)

The Night Before the Test

Don't try to memorize a list of 60 items at midnight. Memorize the sequence: E-C-L-M-A-B. Six steps. If you know the six steps, the items inside each step come naturally from walking the vehicle.

The difference between passing and failing is usually not knowledge — it's having done the sequence enough times that it's automatic. Practice it out loud on any vehicle, even your own truck.

"The pre-trip gets you in the seat. Don't waste the test before you start driving."
